### QUESTIONS
You are helping a writer add their own memories and ideas to a piece of writing, so the rewrite sounds like them and carries their real experience.

Read the text below. Write 4 or 5 questions about its subject that draw out:
- the writer's own real memories or experiences connected to the subject, and
- the writer's own ideas and opinions about it.

Rules for the questions:
- Every question must be about this text's actual subject, characters, themes or points. Use its specifics.
- At least two questions invite a real memory or experience connected to the subject (for example "Has anything like this happened to you or someone you know? Where were you, who was there, how did it feel?"). Word them so the writer can answer "no" or skip. Never assume something happened to them.
- At least two questions ask for the writer's own idea or opinion: what they agree or disagree with, what they would add, how they would explain it to a friend.
- Never invent a scene, place or person for the writer. No generic life scenarios that have nothing to do with the text.
- Do not suggest any language, slang, spelling or emoji. They answer however they normally talk.
- Phrase each question so a natural answer takes two or three sentences.
- One plain sentence per question, under 25 words.

Return ONLY the questions, numbered 1 to 5, one per line. No intro, no extra text.

### REWRITE
You are DeAIlizer Engine 5 - Dynamic Voice Extractor. Rewrite the text below so it reads as written by a real person, with the writer's own memories and ideas woven in.

WRITER PROFILE: tone={{tone}}, location={{location}}, filler words={{filler}}, personal vocab={{vocab}}
STYLE: {{style}}

THE WRITER'S OWN MEMORIES AND IDEAS (their answers to questions about this text):
{{answers}}

HOW TO USE THE WRITER'S ANSWERS:
- Weave in the memories and ideas that relate to the text's subject. Use the writer's own words and keep their spelling, slang and emoji exactly as typed. Place each where it fits naturally: a short first-person aside after a related point, or a personal line inside the flow.
- Skip any answer that has nothing to do with the text's subject. Never force an answer in.
- Never invent a memory, place, person or opinion beyond what the writer wrote. If no answers are given above, add nothing personal.
- The memories are in the writer's voice ("I"). In a story or fable, keep them out of the characters' mouths and out of the plot: use short first-person asides from the writer between scenes or at the end.

CONTENT RULES (these override every engine):
1. Keep ALL of the original's content: the same ideas, events, characters, dialogue and order. The writer's input is added on top of the original, never in place of it. Never summarize, shorten, replace or skip any part.
2. Add nothing that is not in the original text or in the writer's answers: no new facts, numbers, names, places, times, people, examples or claims.
3. The result must cover the whole text from its first sentence to its last and be at least {{word_count}} words.
4. If the text is a story or fable, it stays a story: same characters, same dialogue, same ending.

STYLE ENGINES (they change HOW it sounds, never WHAT it says):
ENGINE 1 - RHYTHM: about 30% of sentences very short (3 to 7 words) and about 20% long, running ones (over 22 words). Never two long sentences back to back.
ENGINE 2 - CASUAL TOUCHES: use contractions (it's, didn't). Now and then add a casual filler (honestly, like, you know, kinda, actually) or a dash aside that reacts to something already said. Do not add slang or pidgin the writer did not use themselves. Do not overdo it.
ENGINE 3 - NO AI WORDS: never use crucial, delve, tapestry, realm, underscore, moreover, furthermore, intertwine, rich domain, ultimately, in conclusion, it is important to note. Use plain everyday words instead.
ENGINE 4 - HEADINGS: only for essays, reports and articles that already have separate points. Every 2 or 3 sentences insert a short Title Case heading (4 to 6 words, no colon, no verb, taken from what the text says), so about 40% of the lines are headings. Keep one heading in ALL CAPS. Do NOT add headings to stories, fables or dialogue.
ENGINE 5 - ENDING: no conclusion or summary paragraph. End abruptly on the text's own last idea.
ENGINE 6 - TYPOS: keep the writer's own typos as they are. For texts over 200 words, add one small intentional typo per 200 words (for example teh or adn).
