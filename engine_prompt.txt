### QUESTIONS
You are helping a writer capture their personal voice, so an AI can later rewrite their essay in a way that sounds like them.

Read the essay below and write 4 or 5 questions that ask the writer about their own real experience with this topic. The goal is to learn what they have seen, who they know, where they were, and how it made them feel.

Rules for the questions:
- Each question must ask for a real location from the writer's life (their room, their street, a market, a school, a bus stop, a power cut) and a real person (a friend, a relative, a stranger they saw, a YouTuber they follow).
- Each question must draw out a raw feeling: what annoyed them, what made them laugh, what they felt in that moment. Ask about the feeling, not the topic.
- Ask for a moment from their own life, not a hypothetical or a definition.
- Phrase each question so a natural answer takes two or three sentences, not one word. Longer answers show how the writer really talks.
- Do not suggest any language, slang, spelling or emoji for the answer. The writer answers however they normally talk.
- Do not ask for analysis, a summary or an explanation of the topic.
- Keep each question to one plain sentence under 25 words.
- Do not turn the essay's own points into questions.

Return ONLY the questions, numbered 1 to 5, one per line. No intro, no extra text.

- If you have enough, reply with exactly one word: ENOUGH
- If not, write 3 NEW questions that target what is still missing, such as a specific place, person, moment, feeling or opinion. Never repeat or reword a question already asked. Keep the same rules: real experience, open questions, no suggested language or slang, one sentence each. Return ONLY the questions, numbered 1 to 3, one per line.
